FluidNinja architecture and buoyancy

How the modified core turns visible water into a surface that floating objects can feel, disturb, and share.

This guide explains the modified FluidNinja LIVE core and the reusable FluidNinja Buoyancy plugin. It connects the implementation to the physics, then walks through building a beach ball, a log, a boat, and a wrecking ball in your own Unreal project. You should finish knowing which settings control weight, floating depth, stability, current, waves, and visual effects.

Core enhancements

Water reacts to objects

Relative tracking, propagating surface waves, independent foam, and optional sharper rendered crests.

Buoyancy extension

Objects react to water

Collision-derived hulls, asynchronous surface sampling, Chaos forces, two-way wave coupling, presets, and diagnostics.

The central idea
The renderer draws water on the GPU. The plugin reads small patches of that water for each body, computes forces in Chaos, and sends the bodies' displacement and impacts back to the modified GPU core.

01 · Ownership and technology

The architecture

FluidNinja owns the GPU flow simulation and water rendering. The core patches extend its Niagara and material assets. The reusable C++ plugin connects that water to Chaos rigid bodies. Your game can consume the plugin's water queries, events, and propulsion controls for its own movement or vehicle systems.

LayerWhere it livesResponsibility
FluidNinja LIVE/Game/FluidNinjaLiveNiagara fluid grid, flow buffers, masks, inputs, surface rendering.
Custom coreModified NinjaLiveCore, painter assets, Waves modules, SURFACE functionRelative tracking; wave, foam, and rendering extensions. Maintained HLSL/tooling lives in the buoyancy plugin.
Shared pluginPlugins/FluidNinjaBuoyancyWater readback, hull physics, self fields, queries, effects, presets, editor validation and developer tests.
Your gameYour own Blueprint or C++ classesOptional consumers of the public APIs. Boarding, payload management, custom movement and network authority are gameplay responsibilities, not automatic buoyancy features.

Technology in plain language. Niagara runs parallel simulation stages on the GPU. HLSL supplies their math and compute shaders. Render targets hold grids of numbers. Unreal's render graph and asynchronous GPU readback move selected results to C++. Chaos integrates forces into rigid-body motion. Blueprints expose the authored objects and gameplay controls. [S4] [S7] [S1]

The runtime connects FluidNinja to Chaos directly. It does not require the FluidFlux solver or Unreal Water's buoyancy component.

02 · Core changes

The FluidNinja core enhancements

The vendor manual for LIVE 2.0 describes buoyancy as unsupported and identifies loss of point identity in its proposed GPU-to-CPU sampling path. The buoyancy plugin keeps per-body patches and identity under its own control. The core modifications add the feedback needed for objects to disturb water without uncontrolled current amplification. Why relative motion matters

The patch adds User.TrackVelocityRelativeToWater. It subtracts the previous fluid velocity from the tracked horizontal motion, with a gate fading from 2 to 10 cm/s, and handles multiple Niagara ticks in one frame. It preserves vertical puncture motion and bypasses screen-position/camera-facing cases. This is a brush interaction model, not a resolved hull boundary in a 3D fluid solver. [S10]

Velocity subtraction alone is insufficient. Tracking must also suppress tracked density using SelectivelyKillBrushDensityKeepVelocity = 1, 4, or 5. Otherwise density can strengthen the river driver. Value 5 also suppresses density from data-channel points. The plugin checks safe settings across NinjaLives, then checks the local interaction volume and object-type/tag selection before handing a body over.

Choose the interaction route deliberately
The FluidNinja Buoyancy component defaults to FluidNinjaTracking, which requires the compatible core and safe tracking settings above. Wakes selects the plugin's point-input route. Applying a preset preserves Interaction unless its Set Interaction flag is enabled. The separate Waves switch controls participation in the physical wave field. [S1] [S12]
03 · Reading water

From a rendered surface to a physics force

The plugin's Surface Mirror is a top-down depth capture restricted to water primitives under relevant body footprints. It follows the actual displaced FluidNinja surface. During this capture, the added wave-height term is disabled and restored, so the capture records the original surface W. The new waves are sampled separately to allow each body's own contribution to be removed.

Surface actors are discovered by tags. The plugin's default Surface Tags are SplineSource, SeaSurface, and FluidNinjaBuoyancySurface. Add the last tag to your pool or lake surface actor, or configure your own tag. These are water surface actor tags, distinct from the primitive tags used by tracking. Steep waterfall faces are rejected because a top-down height capture cannot represent them as ordinary supporting water. [S23]

GPU gather resamples water onto each body's grid. Compatible sources can be grouped, with limits on grid size, bodies, and update schedules. Completed asynchronous readbacks become immutable water patches for physics. Height-rate extrapolation is capped by MaxExtrapolationSeconds (0.08 s by default). It reduces apparent lag; it does not make the coupling instantaneous. [S4] [S15]

Converting FluidNinja flow into physical units

current [cm/s] = buffer velocity × SimSpeed × cell size [cm] × simulation ticks/s × FlowScaleMultiplier

The fixed Niagara tick takes priority over render FPS. For example, at 30 rendered frames/s a core configured for 60 Hz can take two simulation ticks each frame; halving the flow conversion because the display runs at 30 FPS would be wrong. The plugin's default Flow Scale Multiplier is 1. Verify the actual simulation tick and cell size before calibrating current against measured motion. [S4] [S5]

04 · The custom wave model

How objects make waves and feel other waves

A body contributes static head P: the water-depth equivalent of its hull pressing into the surface. Motion of that footprint makes displacement wakes. Entry, drag, and slam contribute transient impulses. The core advances a free surface height η and vertical velocity v alongside FluidNinja's existing flow.

vnext ≈ v − Δt g L(η + P + Pdynamic) − Δt Γv
ηnext = η + Δt vnext
ω² = (gk + (σ/ρ)k³) tanh(kd)

Reading the equations. Gravity restores displaced water; L spreads that restoring influence across wavelengths; Γ represents damping. Wavenumber k = 2π/λ comes from wavelength λ; angular frequency ω = 2πf; d is depth; σ is surface tension. Longer waves and shorter ripples travel differently. In shallow water, long-wave speed approaches √(gd).

The implementation approximates this gravity–capillary dispersion relation with weighted samples from a mip hierarchy. It uses a symplectic Euler update and additional decay, shoreline, edge, and mean-level terms. It is not an FFT ocean and is not a 3D particle or volume fluid. The model is linear surface-wave physics with practical nonlinear damping and source limits. [S7]

At equilibrium under a stationary hull, η tends toward −P, so the drawn term B = η + P tends toward zero instead of leaving a permanent visual hole. Moving that pressure footprint creates traveling waves. Impulse sources are spread spatially, constrained by the forces actually transmitted, and scaled by WaveImpulseScale, currently 0.35. This is calibrated coupling, not an exact water-entry energy budget. [S5] [S8] [S16]

05 · Feedback stability

Why a body does not ride its own delayed wave

If a body presses water down, then feels that response several frames later as a new disturbance, it can feed energy back into its own motion. Each participating body therefore has a local own-wave field: a copy of the wave model driven only by its own sources.

water height felt by a body = W + κ (B − Bself)
κ = project Wave Feel Scale × body Wave Feel Scale

Radiation damping represents energy carried away by the body's own waves. Added mass represents water accelerated along with the body. Both affect heave, roll, and pitch. The current source estimates motion frequency and uses frequency-dependent damping, with a natural-frequency fallback. Added mass works through Stable Steps. The rest-damping boost is 1 in the inspected implementation. [S3] [S6]

A quality tradeoff that affects behavior
A body without a usable own field can still make waves, but does not feel the added wave field. It continues to float on original FluidNinja water. Distance, tile fit, and capacity can cause this fallback. Check it when a distant float looks less responsive.

Foam and choppiness

Interface 4 generates foam from breaking, moving waterline shear, and impacts. Current carries it; exponential decay fades it. With FoamDecay = 0.7, the e-folding time is about 1.43 s: roughly 37% remains after that time, so it is not literally gone in 1.5 seconds. Foam changes appearance only. Separate Niagara whitewater emitters do not automatically consume this foam texture.

WaveChoppy is an optional material static switch, off by default. Its horizontal displacement sharpens crests visually. The physical queries remain based on the height-field model; treat choppiness as an appearance control.

06 · Forces and balance

The physics that makes an object float

In calm water, a floating body displaces its own mass of water. Shape determines where that water is displaced; mass determines how much is needed. The center of buoyancy is the center of the submerged volume. Forces away from the center of mass produce torque, so a boat can pitch, roll, or right itself.

Fbuoyancy = ρwater g Vsubmerged
At rest: Vsubmerged = mass / ρwater
Torque = (force point − center of mass) × force

Keep BuoyancyMultiplier = 1 for this physical relationship. If mass exceeds water density times the full supported volume, the object sinks. A hollow boat can float even though its construction material is denser than water: its sealed outer envelope displaces water while its total mass is much smaller than a solid block of the same size. Flooding and water entering the interior are not simulated. [S2] [Archimedes]

Three hull representations

Hull modelCalculationUse it for
TrianglesSubdivides simple collision, clips faces at the waterline, integrates hydrostatic pressure, drag, and slam over wet faces.Boats and shaped hulls; logs when surface fidelity matters.
VoxelsFills collision with cubes and integrates their wet portions.Crates, blocks, and approximate debris. Voxel Count trades detail for cost.
SpheresUses exact submerged spherical caps for pontoons; projected/wetted geometry for resistance.Balls and simple support points. Sphere rows/rings are approximations, not unioned solids.

The physics hull comes from simple collision, not the beautiful render mesh. Avoid overlapping collision pieces: they can double-count volume, lift, and drag. A closed boat envelope should contain the volume it keeps dry. A thin shell that only follows wood thickness will displace too little; several overlapping boxes can displace too much.

Drag and impact

Body-point velocity includes translation and rotation: vpoint = vCOM + ω × r. Subtract local water velocity to get motion through water. The plugin uses pressure and suction terms with linear and quadratic speed coefficients, angle falloff, ITTC skin friction, and slam on growing wet area. These coefficients are tuning controls for the implemented face model; they are not a single universal drag coefficient copied from a textbook. The familiar physical scale is ½ρ CD A v². [S2] [Drag equation]

A heavier object does not automatically fall faster. Ignoring air resistance, entry speed from height H is √(2gH). It does carry more momentum mv and energy ½mv². Slam, entry power, visible splash, and traveling wave amplitude are related but separately modeled.

07 · Stable behavior

Mass, draft, and stability are different controls

ControlMeaningChoose it when
Body MassUses the primitive's kg. Solves the floating depth from that mass.You know the actual object's weight or want it to sink realistically.
Rest DraftAt setup, sets mass equal to displaced water at this depth and Rest Attitude.You are authoring a desired waterline. The primitive's previous mass is replaced.
Preset DensityEditor preset computes mass from collision volume × density, then uses Body Mass.You want similarly modeled objects of one material to scale in weight.
Center Of Mass OffsetMoves the mass center and changes restoring moments.The waterline is correct but the object rolls or tips unrealistically.
Inertia ScaleChanges resistance to angular acceleration.The mass distribution of a hollow body differs from its collision solid.

Do not repair bad geometry with lift gain. First check units, scale, collision volume, mass mode, and draft. Then adjust center of mass and the collision envelope. Drag controls how motion settles; it should not be the main way to conceal a statically unstable boat.

The small-angle stability test

GM = KB + Iwaterplane / Vsubmerged − KG

GM is metacentric height: a small-angle measure of whether a tilted body tends to return upright. KB is height of the buoyancy center above the hull bottom; KG is height of the mass center; I is the waterplane's second moment of area about the roll or pitch axis. Positive GM suggests restoring torque near that attitude; negative GM suggests tipping. It does not predict every large-angle capsize or collision. Analyze Hull reports roll and pitch stability for the modeled hull. [S2]

Why light bodies need Stable Steps

A light ball can gain a large amount of buoyant force after a tiny vertical movement. Its effective water spring is stiff compared with its mass. An explicit force step that is too long can overshoot and add energy. The plugin's Stable Steps handles the unresolved part of stiffness and drag implicitly and includes added mass, rather than demanding one global frame rate for every object.

For intuition: stiffness K ≈ ρgAwaterplane
Natural period T ≈ 2π √((m + added mass) / K)

Small mass and large waterplane area give a short period. A physics step must resolve that period. Leave Stable Steps on for ordinary use; compare fnb.Buoyancy.Implicit 0 and 1 only as a diagnostic, then restore -1 to use project policy. Test the intended frame rate and physics settings in your project; a particular asynchronous physics rate is not a universal prerequisite. [S3] [S5]

Tune in this order
Collision and scale → physical mass or intended draft → balance and inertia → drag and current → wave coupling → splash, foam, and sound. Recheck after changing hull resolution because the discrete force and splash samples change.
08 · Implementation

Create the first floating object

Use a small calm test area before adding a river, waves, or character interactions. These steps describe the current editor workflow; they do not modify your project automatically.

  1. Validate the water setup. Enable the compiled FluidNinja Buoyancy plugin and the matching modified FluidNinja core. Run Tools → FluidNinja Buoyancy: Check Setup or fnb.CheckSetup. Read each finding, especially surface discovery, core interface, buffers, and interaction policy.
  2. Configure shared settings. Open Project Settings → Game → FluidNinja Buoyancy. Start with the plugin defaults, including fresh-water density 1000 kg/m³, Flow Scale Multiplier 1, and Stable Steps on.
  3. Identify the water surface. Tag its actor using a configured Surface Tag. Confirm the material uses the right FluidNinja velocity/pressure buffers and simulation mapping. A mesh merely looking blue is not enough.
  4. Prepare a body. Create an Actor Blueprint with a movable Static Mesh Component as root. Give it suitable simple collision, enable physics/collision, and use CCD for small fast drop objects. Prefer a mesh authored at its intended dimensions with unit component scale for the first test.
  5. Add one buoyancy component. Add FluidNinja Buoyancy to the actor. If the floating primitive is not root, set PrimitiveName to its exact component name. Use one buoyancy component per simulated body.
  6. Apply a recipe. Use a preset through the component's Preset panel, or right-click the placed actor → FluidNinja Buoyancy → Apply Preset. Enable Show Plugin Content to find /FluidNinjaBuoyancy/Presets. The actor-level tool can add a buoyancy component when one is missing.
  7. Set mass and starting behavior. Use Body Mass for measured kg or density presets, Rest Draft for a chosen waterline. Leave Settle On Start on for an already floating object; turn it off for a drop. Keep Buoyancy Multiplier and Flow Coupling at 1 for the initial physical baseline.
  8. Analyze and test. Click Analyze Hull. Read volume, mass, draft, and GM. In Play use fnb.Buoyancy 2 and Get Readout. Check that a settled fresh-water floater displaces about one litre per kilogram, then test a gentle push and a small drop.
  9. Add feedback and presentation. Confirm Waves is enabled for the body and simulation. Choose Wakes or safe tracking deliberately. Add the wetness material function and configure splash/sounds only after the body behaves correctly.
Apply hull presets before Play
Preset application writes properties and mass. It does not promise to rebuild an already initialized runtime hull. Make structural changes in the editor and restart Play. Use Apply Buoyancy Preset To Component when you need to target a particular existing component. [S12]

When gameplay teleports a floating actor, call MarkTeleported() after moving it. This lets the wave system erase the old footprint and reset the own field instead of treating the move as a violent water displacement.

11 · Displacement and sinking

A small boat and a wrecking ball

A boat with an explicitly defined displacement hull

Use a closed triangular prism as a teaching hull: length 300 cm, beam 120 cm, keel-to-gunwale height 50 cm. At each end X = ±150 cm, the Y/Z cross-section vertices are (0,0), (−60,50), and (60,50). Close the top. The pivot is at the keel midpoint. This sealed envelope models excluded water; it does not simulate flooding.

V(d) = L B d² / (2H)
d = √(2H m / (ρ L B)) = 25.8199 cm
Full volume = LBH/2 = 900 L

Use Triangles, resolution 20–30 cm, budget 1000, Body Mass 240 kg. Settle On Start on, Waves on, Wake on, propulsion off for the first hydrostatic test, and Splash Min Watts 300. Target a center of mass 20 cm above the keel. If Chaos starts at the uniform prism centroid 33.333 cm above keel, COM offset Z = −13.333 cm achieves that at unit scale; verify the actual COM before copying the offset.

Ideal roll GM is about 22.0 cm for that mass center. An extra centered 80 kg gives total mass 320 kg and draft 29.814 cm, about 3.99 cm deeper. A rider placed to one side changes both load and balance. The flat-ended prism has unusually strong pitch stability; it is an explanatory hull, not a prediction for all dinghies.

The public BoatHull preset chooses 13 cm Rest Draft and 30 cm triangles. On this V-hull, 13 cm corresponds to only 60.84 kg. A draft preset is not a universal boat mass. Use total known boat-and-payload kg for a real vessel.

An 80 cm solid steel wrecking ball

Use matching sphere geometry/collision, one pontoon at the origin with radius 40 cm and both weights 1. Set Body Mass to 2,104.448 kg for the chosen steel density 7,850 kg/m³. Turn Settle On Start off, enable CCD, keep Waves on, Wake on, and use Splash Min Watts 20 as a starting visual floor.

They sit near the stern and below its calculated 25.82 cm waterline. For your mesh, use its actual geometry, pivot, and waterline instead.
  3. Keep Thrust Direction +X. Start with Throttle and Rudder at zero. Map a forward/reverse input to Throttle and a left/right input to Rudder; reset them to zero when input ends. Use Console Control off for this gameplay path.
  4. Test a small throttle, then steering while moving. Verify the forces disappear when their own points leave the water. A dry rudder does not steer just because the hull is floating.
Fthrust = Throttle × MaxThrustNewtons × direction
U = dot(vbody point − vcurrent, forward) + Throttle × PropWashSpeed
CL = clamp(2π × rudder angle in radians, −1.2, 1.2)
Frudder = −right × ½ρ A CL U |U|
Torque = (force point − center of mass) × force

The rudder uses the point's linear and angular body motion, horizontal current scaled by Flow Coupling, and propeller wash. Convert area to m² and speed to m/s if evaluating this equation in SI units. At the default 1500 N maximum, Throttle 0.2 commands 300 N when the propeller point is wet. For the 240 kg example, F/m is 1.25 m/s² before drag, added-mass response, and other forces. It is a force estimate, not a promised acceleration or top speed. [S2] [S18]

The rudder assumes body +X forward
Thrust Direction can change the propeller direction, but the built-in rudder still uses the body's X/Y axes. Rotating that one property does not rotate the rudder model. Also, the default force points are at the origin: move them to meaningful locations or they may be dry or provide little turning leverage.

For console testing, enable Console Control and use fnb.Boat.Throttle 0.2 and fnb.Boat.Rudder 0.25, then set both to 0. Changes affect all bodies with Propulsion and Console Control enabled, not only the selected boat. [S4]

Seats, boarding, animation-driven oars, passenger loading, and network authority belong to your game. Include payload in the total mass and balance used by your hull setup, and test changes against the intended waterline. The plugin's propeller-and-rudder controls do not automatically add a passenger's mass or implement those gameplay systems.

Entry and slam are now separate

The C++ force integrator collects growing wet area across the body before distributing its slam, with an inward-speed gate of 50 cm/s. A mass-limited force prevents a piecewise water-entry impulse from arbitrarily stopping more than its supported share of the body in one step.

G = Σ(dAi/dt), Q = Σ((dAi/dt) vi), v̄ = Q/G
Fslam = min[SlamMultiplier ρ Q beam, (m v̄/Δt) min(2Aparticipating/Ahull, 1)]
Pentry = m vdown³ / (2Hbody)
Splash strength ≈ (power / SplashReferenceWatts)1/3

Entry power is a visual estimate of kinetic energy delivered over an entry time; it is not an extra physical force. The default reference is 70 W; body and project ceilings normally cap strength at 12 before the final art scale. Twice the visible scale requires eight times the power before those caps. Splash Min Watts gates repeated slam splashes; setting it to zero does not disable entry splashes or bow spray. [S2] [S5]

For more white wake: first tune interface-4 foam or its material strength. Point-wake gains inject into FluidNinja's flow/density interaction and can alter river speed. Neither a symmetric burst nor a gain below 1 guarantees conservation in the full nonlinear flow setup. Compare mean current before and after adding interacting bodies when calibrating this route.

Hull wetness and sound

Wet Line writes four contiguous Custom Primitive Data slots starting at WetLineDataIndex (default 0). The hull material needs MF_FNB_HullWetness or equivalent water-plane CPD integration. Enabling the property alone does not change an arbitrary material. Reserve those slots to avoid collisions with other material features. Drying Speed defaults to 1.5 cm/s. [S1] [S18]

The plugin exposes configurable splash and sound assets. Assign the assets appropriate to your project; slap, wash, creak, and optional drip have separate roles. Check visibility distance and global effect budgets when physics is correct but spray is missing.

  Get Water At Location(position) → success, sample

    check bFromPatch to distinguish capture from far-field height

For swimmers, SurfaceZ = StillZ + η, with η = pressure.B − pressure.A. Depth = SurfaceZ − query.Z is negative above water. Current is horizontal; its Z component is zero. The returned normal comes from the wave slope. A query can use an existing body's capture patch, otherwise a known far-field water surface. An arbitrary point query does not create a fresh whole-scene capture by itself. [S13]

The plugin supports swimmer registration and a FluidNinja Swimmer component for body-part contacts, displacement, and wave impulses. For a custom movement system, implement IFluidNinjaSwimmerProvider::GetSwimmerPose, a Blueprint-native interface event, or derive a swimmer component in C++. A swimmer profile configures its body-part contacts. The plain C++ virtual state hooks are not automatically Blueprint override events. Wind is supplied through the plugin's world-space Wind setting in cm/s, with zero wind by default; your weather system can provide that input. [S24] [S25] [S5]

Readout is diagnostic, not the final net force
ForceN reports raw hydrodynamic integration. The callback subsequently adjusts forces for radiation damping, stable steps, and added mass. Gravity and character contacts are separate. Do not infer exact body acceleration by dividing this readout by mass.

Not every property marked editable has a Blueprint setter. The explicit runtime controls include Throttle, Rudder, Interaction, Waves, and Wave Feel Scale; use the declared APIs. Hull rebuilding and arbitrary live preset swapping are not established by these headers.

17 · Mathematical reference

What the force integrator computes

This section links the simple Archimedes explanation to the implementation. Use consistent units when evaluating formulas. The public density is kg/m³; the integrator converts it to kg/cm³, and converts force and power back to N and W for readouts.

Triangle pressure and center of pressure

Fpiece = −n ρ g h A × BuoyancyMultiplier
D = d₁ + d₂ + d₃
rpressure = [(D+d₁)r₁ + (D+d₂)r₂ + (D+d₃)r₃] / (4D)

A triangle is clipped at the sampled waterline. For each wet piece, n is outward normal, A is area, h is mean depth, and dᵢ are vertex depths. Pressure grows linearly with depth. The depth-weighted point matters: applying every force at the triangle centroid can give the wrong righting torque even when total lift is correct. [S2]

Triangle pressure drag and skin friction

s = |vrelative| / (1 m/s), q₀ = ½ρ(1 m/s)²
c = n · normalize(vrelative)
For c > 0: Fpressure = −n q₀ A (Cp1s + Cp2s²)cfp
For c ≤ 0: Fsuction = +n q₀ A (Cs1s + Cs2s²)(−c)fs
Cfriction = 0.075 / (log₁₀ Re − 2)²

Skin friction opposes tangential velocity with a ½ρC A v² scale. Reynolds number uses hull length and viscosity, with a minimum of 100,000 in this implementation. Spheres and voxels use a projected-section drag approximation rather than the triangle suction/falloff model. Their geometry scale means a textbook sphere drag coefficient is not automatically a calibrated terminal-speed setting here.

Spheres and voxels

Sphere h = clamp(water Z − center Z + R, 0, 2R)
Vcap = πh²(3R − h)/3
Voxel wet fraction = clamp((water Z − center Z)/cell size + ½, 0, 1)

A sphere's lift is ρgVcap times LiftWeight and the buoyancy multiplier. Explicit pontoons are summed, not Boolean-unioned. Voxels share the collision volume across retained interior samples; their simple vertical wet fraction is not exact rotated cube clipping. The triangle surface is still retained for wave-source displacement, so a sphere/voxel lift model should remain consistent with collision geometry.

Implicit treatment of unresolved response

Δv = Δt (F + W + A awater − θ Δt Kexcess v)
÷ (m + A + Δt Cexcess + θ Δt² Kexcess)

This heave expression shows Stable Steps with excess stiffness K, excess damping C, added mass A, signed gravitational force W (negative in upward-positive Z), and filtered water acceleration. θ defaults to 1. The actual force is rewritten so Chaos reaches this velocity change using its own body mass. Analogous inertia/torque handling applies to rotation. Stiffness and damping already resolved by the step are left explicit. Stability can include numerical damping; it is not a guarantee of identical motion at every frame rate. [S2] [S3]

The sampled height rate helps predict height but is not used as vertical drag-current velocity; that current remains horizontal. Added mass has a separate filtered water-acceleration estimate. This distinction prevents abrupt visual height changes from becoming spurious vertical drag kicks.

  2. Run the editor setup checker, then close the editor before the core tool's commandlet verify or patch workflow. Require its report to identify the core and finish RESULT PASS; read warnings about older capabilities.
  3. For a new vendor version, first port the relative-tracking graph changes. The tool's tracking step is not implemented; it cannot automatically turn a pristine vendor core into the required safe base.
  4. From the supported relative-tracking base, follow the tool's core material verify workflow. The core step refuses a core already carrying waves; use the documented reapplication/backup procedure rather than blindly applying twice.
  5. Perform material changes in a fresh commandlet/editor process before PIE, following the patch tool's workflow.
  6. Open the intended level, validate surfaces and sim assignments, then run settling, drift, entry, neighbor-wave, and intended-quality tests. Keep the compile and test reports with your recorded version information.
